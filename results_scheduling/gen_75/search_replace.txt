<adaptive_local_search_window>
This modification introduces an adaptive local machine search window for operations based on their job's priority. High-priority jobs (CRITICAL/HIGH) will continue to search the full time horizon for the earliest and cheapest local slot, leveraging local machines for cost efficiency. However, for lower-priority jobs (MEDIUM/LOW), the local search window is truncated. If a local slot isn't immediately available within a short lookahead window, these operations will more quickly fall back to cloud resources.

This strategy aims to:
1.  **Prioritize critical workloads**: By not letting lower-priority jobs hold up local resources for extended search periods, critical jobs have a better chance of securing local machines.
2.  **Reduce overall delay**: For lower-priority jobs, a shorter local search window means they will be scheduled on the cloud faster if local capacity is not readily available, thus avoiding prolonged delays that could accumulate penalty points. This trades off potentially higher compute cost for reduced delay penalties.
3.  **Improve local resource utilization for high-value tasks**: Local machines are more likely to be available for high-priority jobs which can justify waiting for a local slot due to their importance and cost sensitivity.

The new `MEDIUM_PRIORITY_LOCAL_SEARCH_WINDOW` and `LOW_PRIORITY_LOCAL_SEARCH_WINDOW` parameters control how far ahead the scheduler looks for local slots for respective job priorities.

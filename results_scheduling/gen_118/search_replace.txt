<adaptive_local_search_window_refined>
This modification refines the local machine scheduling strategy by implementing a more sophisticated adaptive search window and ensuring a comprehensive search within that window.

The key improvements are:
1.  **Adaptive Search Window based on Job Slack**: The `search_end_time` for local machines is now dynamically adjusted based on the job's urgency (slack).
    *   New constants `SLACK_THRESHOLD`, `TIGHT_WINDOW_MULTIPLIER`, `NORMAL_WINDOW_MULTIPLIER`, and `MAX_ABSOLUTE_SEARCH_WINDOW` are introduced.
    *   For jobs with minimal or negative slack (tight deadlines), the search window for local machines is narrowed (`TIGHT_WINDOW_MULTIPLIER`). This prioritizes finding an immediate or very near-term slot to avoid escalating delay penalties.
    *   For jobs with ample slack, the search window is expanded (`NORMAL_WINDOW_MULTIPLIER`), allowing the scheduler to look further into the future for cheaper off-peak hours, thereby reducing `compute_cost`.
    *   The `search_end_time` calculation now considers the job's deadline, a dynamic multiplier of the `time_horizon` based on slack, and an absolute maximum search limit, ensuring a balanced and efficient search.
2.  **Removal of Premature Break**: The `break` statement inside the local machine search loop has been removed. The previous implementation only found the *earliest valid* start time for an operation on a given local server. By removing this `break`, the algorithm now iterates through all possible `start_time_candidate`s within the dynamically defined window, allowing it to identify a potentially later, but significantly cheaper, slot that minimizes the overall objective cost (compute cost + delay penalty). This ensures that the algorithm truly finds the best cost-time trade-off within its search horizon.

These changes together make the local scheduling decision more informed and robust, especially under varying job urgency and cost dynamics, leading to a better overall score by optimizing the cost-time tradeoff.

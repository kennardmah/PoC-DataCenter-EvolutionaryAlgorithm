<priority_adaptive_dispatch>
Enhances the `PlacementEvaluator` by integrating a non-linear load balancing penalty (quadratic) and a proactive hotspot/capacity preservation penalty. This helps to avoid severe resource contention on local machines, especially critical during emergency scenarios, while still balancing cost and delay for different job priorities. The parameters for search horizon and cloud aggressiveness are also refined based on priority.
</NAME>

<DESCRIPTION>
This edit introduces two key improvements to the `_calculate_score` function within the `PlacementEvaluator` class:

1.  **Non-Linear Load Balancing:** The existing load penalty for local servers is modified from a linear function of average utilization to a quadratic function. This change makes the penalty for increasing server utilization more aggressive, particularly when a server is already heavily loaded. This discourages scheduling on already congested machines, leading to better distribution of workload and preventing hotspots, especially crucial during potential machine failures in emergency scenarios (Recommendation 2).

2.  **Proactive Hotspot/Capacity Preservation Penalty:** A new penalty `hotspot_penalty` is added. This penalty activates if placing the current operation would cause the local server's remaining capacity (after the operation is scheduled) to fall below a predefined `hotspot_capacity_threshold_ratio` during the operation's duration. The penalty scales quadratically with the depth of the violation, with a very high additional penalty if capacity drops to zero or becomes negative. This proactively preserves a minimum reserve capacity on local machines, preventing critical resource depletion and ensuring that capacity is available for unexpected urgent or emergency jobs (Recommendation 1, refined as a hotspot penalty).

Additionally, the `priority_base_horizon` in `find_best_placement` is slightly re-tuned to be more aggressive (shorter search horizon) for higher-priority jobs and allow a longer search for lower-priority ones, further emphasizing urgency. The `priority_load_multiplier` and `cloud_aggressiveness_threshold` are also adjusted to reflect a strategy where high-priority jobs are less penalized for contributing to load (allowing them to push through) and are slightly more willing to use local if available, while low-priority jobs are heavily penalized by load and more readily offloaded to cloud. These parameter adjustments aim to improve the balance between cost, delay, and resilience across various job priorities and scenarios, particularly during emergencies.
</DESCRIPTION>

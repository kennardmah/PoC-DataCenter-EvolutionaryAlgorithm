<load_balancing_refactor>
Refactor load balancing to aggressively avoid hotspots and low remaining capacity.
</NAME>

<DESCRIPTION>
This edit refactors the load balancing penalty within the `_calculate_operation_score` function to be more proactive and sensitive to potential bottlenecks. It introduces two main components:

1.  **Enhanced Non-Linear High Utilization Penalty:** Replaces the previous quadratic penalty with a cubic function (`(over_utilization ** 3) * factor`) for utilization above a `high_utilization_threshold` (0.7). This makes the penalty significantly sharper and more aggressive as server utilization climbs, discouraging scheduling on already heavily loaded machines.
2.  **Distinct Critical Capacity Buffer Penalty:** Adds a new, strong fixed penalty if the proposed operation causes the server's remaining capacity to drop below a `critical_capacity_threshold_ratio` (0.20) of its total available capacity at any point in the `hotspot_lookahead_window`. This explicitly prevents the scheduler from filling a machine to near-full capacity, maintaining a buffer for unforeseen events or other high-priority jobs.

These changes aim to prevent local servers from becoming overloaded "hotspots" and to ensure a minimum capacity buffer, which is crucial for resilience, especially in emergency scenarios. The penalties are also scaled by the job's `urgency_score` (derived from `JobUrgencyProfile`), making high-priority jobs more sensitive to server congestion. The old `hotspot_penalty_factor` is removed as its functionality is superseded by the new, more granular penalty factors.
</DESCRIPTION>

<DESCRIPTION>
This edit performs a comprehensive correction and tuning of the scheduling heuristics. The previous implementation had several logical flaws that severely hampered its performance, especially for high-priority and emergency jobs.

1.  **Corrected Priority Logic:** All heuristic parameter dictionaries (`priority_base_horizon`, `priority_procrastination_multiplier`, `cloud_aggressiveness_threshold`, `priority_load_multiplier`) were missing entries for `'emergency'` jobs. This edit adds them, ensuring these critical jobs receive appropriate, aggressive scheduling.

2.  **Fixed Monotonicity:** The `priority_load_multiplier` dictionary was not ordered correctly by priority (e.g., 'critical' had a lower multiplier than 'urgent'). This has been fixed to ensure that sensitivity to server load correctly increases with job priority.

3.  **Inverted Cloud Aggressiveness:** The `cloud_aggressiveness_threshold` logic was backward. High-priority jobs were incorrectly configured to prefer expensive local slots, while low-priority jobs were pushed to the cloud. This has been inverted: high-priority jobs now strongly prefer the cloud to avoid delays (threshold < 1.0), while low-priority jobs will wait for much cheaper local capacity (threshold > 1.0).

4.  **Introduced Non-Linear Load Penalty:** The load-balancing penalty was changed from a linear `(avg_utilization)` to a non-linear `(avg_utilization ** 2)` function. This drastically penalizes scheduling on highly congested servers, a proven strategy for improving throughput and reducing delays by avoiding bottlenecks. The associated `load_penalty_factor` was increased to give this more powerful penalty appropriate weight.

5.  **General Tuning:** Other parameters, like `base_procrastination_penalty_factor`, were fine-tuned to better balance the trade-offs between waiting for cheaper slots and scheduling immediately to avoid delay penalties.

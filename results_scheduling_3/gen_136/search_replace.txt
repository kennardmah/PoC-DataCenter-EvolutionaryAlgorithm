<load_and_contention_penalty>
<DESCRIPTION>
This edit refines the load balancing and contention penalty mechanism within the `_calculate_operation_score` method.

1.  **Inverted Priority Load Multiplier:** The `priority_load_multiplier` is inverted. Previously, emergency jobs had a low multiplier, meaning they incurred *less* penalty for busy servers. This is counter-intuitive; high-priority jobs should be *more* sensitive to congestion to ensure their timely completion and avoid further delays. The change makes high-priority jobs (Emergency, Critical) incur higher penalties for placing operations on highly utilized servers, encouraging them to seek less congested paths or leverage cloud resources. Lower priority jobs will now incur lower penalties for congestion, making them more likely to fill available, but potentially busier, local slots.

2.  **Aggressive Non-Linear Load Penalty:** The load penalty calculation for local machines is significantly enhanced:
    *   **Direct Capacity Impact:** Instead of an average utilization over a wide window, the penalty now focuses on the *projected utilization* for each specific time slot the operation will occupy.
    *   **Tiered Non-Linearity:**
        *   A moderate linear penalty is applied for utilization between 50% and 80%.
        *   A much more aggressive, exponential penalty is applied for utilization above 80%, steeply escalating as capacity approaches 100%. This strongly discourages placing operations in heavily congested slots, preventing bottlenecks and improving flow.
    *   **Proactive Hotspot Avoidance:** A separate, less aggressive quadratic penalty is retained for the `hotspot_lookahead_window` immediately *after* the operation finishes. This encourages avoiding placements that would create immediate future bottlenecks for subsequent operations or other jobs.

**Argumentation:**
-   **Priority-Sensitive Load Avoidance:** By inverting the `priority_load_multiplier`, high-priority jobs are actively incentivized to avoid congested local resources. This is critical for emergency scenarios, where rapid, unimpeded execution is paramount. Forcing them into busy queues by applying *low* load penalties would defeat their priority. This change aligns with the principle that high-priority tasks should have preferential access to uncongested resources, even if it means higher cost (cloud) in a pinch.
-   **Enhanced Load Balancing and Contention:** The new tiered, non-linear load penalty directly addresses both Recommendation 1 (aggressive, non-linear load balancing) and Recommendation 4 (proactive inter-job contention penalty).
    *   The direct slot-by-slot evaluation with exponential penalties effectively acts as an "inter-job contention penalty" by making slots already heavily used by *other* jobs extremely expensive. This incentivizes the scheduler to spread jobs across available capacity.
    *   The aggressive nature of the penalty, particularly for high utilization, forces the scheduler to make harder decisions: either find a less utilized local machine or resort to cloud, rather than simply accepting a slightly delayed slot on a choked resource.
    *   The proactive lookahead penalty helps prevent "chain reactions" of congestion, where placing one operation now creates a block for others immediately afterward.

This comprehensive update to the cost function's load component is expected to improve resource utilization, reduce delays for high-priority jobs, and make the schedule more robust to failures by spreading load more intelligently.

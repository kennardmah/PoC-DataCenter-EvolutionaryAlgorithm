<DESCRIPTION>
This edit introduces a "Slack Crunch Penalty" to the operation scoring function. The primary motivation is to prevent myopic, greedy scheduling decisions for an early operation that negatively impact the scheduling flexibility of subsequent operations within the same job.

Currently, the scheduler might place an operation on a local server after a short wait, which appears optimal in isolation. However, this delay could consume all the available buffer time (slack) for the rest of the job's operations, forcing them onto expensive cloud servers or causing deadline violations.

The new penalty function works as follows:
1.  For a potential placement (`server_id`, `start_time`), it calculates the `remaining_slack` for all subsequent operations of the job.
2.  It defines a `critical_buffer` (e.g., 33% of the total duration of subsequent operations). This buffer represents a desirable amount of scheduling flexibility.
3.  If the `remaining_slack` for a given placement falls below this `critical_buffer`, a non-linear (quadratic) penalty is applied.
4.  The penalty's magnitude scales with the severity of the "crunch" (how far below the buffer the slack is), the job's delay weight, and its priority.

This proactive penalty encourages the scheduler to select placements that preserve flexibility for the entire job, leading to better overall solutions by balancing the cost of the current operation against the potential future costs for subsequent operations. It directly makes the scheduler "aware" of the downstream consequences of its decisions for each job.
</DESCRIPTION>

<adaptive_local_search_window>
This edit implements an adaptive local search window for local machines based on job priority. Previously, the search window was a fixed 48 hours. The change introduces `DEFAULT_LOCAL_SEARCH_WINDOW_HOURS` and `PRIORITY_WINDOW_MULTIPLIERS` to dynamically adjust the search horizon. Higher priority jobs ('emergency', 'high') will now search a wider range of future slots on local machines, while lower priority jobs ('low') will have a more constrained search.

This approach aims to:
1.  **Reduce Cloud Usage for Critical Jobs**: By expanding the search window for high-priority jobs, the scheduler has a better chance of finding a suitable (even if slightly later) local slot, potentially avoiding more expensive cloud resources. This aligns with the "delay penalty" being high for critical jobs, but the cost of cloud being even higher.
2.  **Improve Resource Utilization**: Allowing critical jobs to look further ahead can help them secure optimal local machine slots, preventing them from immediately spilling over to cloud due to short-term local congestion, and thus freeing up local capacity for other jobs in the short term.
3.  **Balance Cost and Urgency**: For non-critical jobs, a shorter search window keeps the scheduling decisions more agile and prevents them from excessively blocking future capacity for higher priority work by reserving far-future slots.

The chosen multipliers (1.5x for emergency, 1.25x for high) expand the window beyond the previous fixed `inst.time_horizon * 2 + 12 = 60` for `high` and `emergency` priorities, leveraging an observed effective range from past programs while `medium` and `low` remain within or below the original value, balancing search complexity with priority.

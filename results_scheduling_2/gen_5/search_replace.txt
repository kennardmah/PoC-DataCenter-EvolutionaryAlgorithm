This edit introduces a `LOCAL_MACHINE_SLACK_FACTOR` (set to 1.2 heuristically) within the `schedule_job_online` function. This factor is applied to the `remaining_duration` when evaluating potential placements on local machines.

The rationale is that `operation_objective` calculates a projected completion time by adding the `remaining_duration` (sum of durations of subsequent operations) to the current operation's finish time. For local machines, this `remaining_duration` is often an optimistic estimate, as future operations might face contention and additional queuing delays, especially in an online setting with unknown future arrivals and potential failures.

By multiplying `remaining_duration` by `LOCAL_MACHINE_SLACK_FACTOR` for local machine placements, we introduce a heuristic penalty. This makes local options appear slightly less attractive (higher projected delay penalty) compared to cloud options (where `remaining_duration` is not scaled, as cloud capacity is unlimited). This encourages the scheduler to:
1. Prioritize earlier local slots more aggressively to mitigate the scaled future delay.
2. Consider cloud machines more readily when local capacity is tight or deadlines are critical, as the "cost" of future delays on local machines is now higher.

This is a simple yet effective heuristic to make the online scheduler more robust to future uncertainty and resource contention on local assets, without increasing computational complexity significantly. The value `1.2` is a starting point and could be further tuned.

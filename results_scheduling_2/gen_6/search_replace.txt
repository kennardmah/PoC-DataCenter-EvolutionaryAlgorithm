The original scheduler for local machines was purely greedy: it picked the *first* available slot it found. This might not be optimal because later slots might have lower hourly costs or result in a smaller total delay penalty, even if they are not the absolute earliest.

This edit introduces a `LOOKAHEAD_WINDOW_LOCAL` parameter (set to 4 hours). For local machines, instead of taking the very first available slot, the scheduler now searches for the slot within `current_start` and `current_start + LOOKAHEAD_WINDOW_LOCAL` that yields the minimum `operation_objective` cost for that specific machine. If no slot is found within this window, it still falls back to searching for the *first* available slot beyond the lookahead window, but within a reasonable upper bound to ensure the job can be scheduled. This balances finding a better spot on local machines with the online constraint of limited knowledge and avoids excessive computational load. Cloud options are still evaluated for immediate start as they have unlimited capacity.

By looking ahead a small window, the scheduler can potentially find cheaper or less penalizing slots on local machines, leading to better overall scores, especially in scenarios with varying costs or tight deadlines.

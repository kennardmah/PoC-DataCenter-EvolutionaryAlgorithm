<priority_adaptive_scheduling>
<DESCRIPTION>
This edit refines the online scheduling heuristic by introducing two priority-aware mechanisms:

1.  **Priority-Adaptive Local Slot Search Window**: The `SEARCH_WINDOW_LIMIT` (which dictates how far into the future the scheduler looks for available slots on local machines) is dynamically adjusted based on the job's priority.
    *   Higher priority jobs (`Emergency`, `High`) are given a shorter search window. This encourages them to be scheduled sooner, either on an immediate local slot or by quickly resorting to a cloud machine to minimize critical delays.
    *   Lower priority jobs (`Low`) are given a longer search window. This allows them to explore more future slots on local machines, potentially finding cheaper options further out, as their deadlines are typically more flexible.
    *   A `base_search_window_limit` is established using a fraction of the `inst.time_horizon` to provide a reasonable default context-aware search range.

2.  **Pessimistic Remaining Duration for Local Operations**: When evaluating a placement for the *current* operation on a local machine, a small, fixed `LOCAL_FUTURE_OP_DELAY_PENALTY_PER_OP` (e.g., 0.5 hours) is added to the `remaining_duration` for each *subsequent* operation.
    *   This heuristic implicitly accounts for the increased uncertainty, contention, and potential for failures associated with relying on local resources for a job's subsequent operations.
    *   By slightly inflating the projected completion time for local options, it subtly biases the objective function towards cloud resources or local slots that lead to a faster overall job completion, especially for jobs with many remaining operations, thereby mitigating future delay risks.

These changes aim to improve the balance between cost optimization and deadline adherence, especially enhancing resilience and responsiveness for high-priority jobs in dynamic, failure-prone environments, while allowing more cost-efficient scheduling for lower-priority tasks.

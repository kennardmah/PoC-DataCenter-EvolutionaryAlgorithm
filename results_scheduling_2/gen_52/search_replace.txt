<DESCRIPTION>
This edit introduces an additional heuristic to the scheduling decision-making process: a `future_work_penalty`. The core idea is to encourage the scheduler to prioritize operations that belong to jobs with a significant amount of *future* work remaining. This helps to 'eat into' longer jobs sooner, potentially reducing overall makespan and mitigating the risk of future delays, especially for high-priority jobs.

The `future_work_penalty` is calculated as:
`_remaining_ops_duration * FUTURE_WORK_PENALTY_RATE_BASE * priority_factor`

-   `_remaining_ops_duration`: The sum of durations of all subsequent operations for the current job. This provides a direct measure of the workload still to be processed for this job.
-   `FUTURE_WORK_PENALTY_RATE_BASE`: A new configurable parameter (e.g., `0.1`) that sets the base cost per hour of remaining work. This allows tuning the influence of future work on the current decision. A higher value will make the scheduler more aggressive about tackling longer jobs earlier.
-   `priority_factor`: The existing priority multiplier for the job. By applying this factor, the penalty for outstanding future work becomes proportionally higher for high-priority and emergency jobs, further incentivizing their faster completion and resource allocation.

This enhancement aims to improve the online dispatch heuristic by:
1.  **Proactive Workload Management**: Instead of only reacting to imminent deadlines or current compute costs, the scheduler now has a soft incentive to progress jobs with substantial remaining work.
2.  **Prioritization of Long-Running Jobs**: Jobs composed of many operations or very long operations will incur a higher `future_work_penalty`, making their current operations more attractive to schedule, especially for high-priority tasks.
3.  **Improved Resilience**: By completing more operations earlier, the schedule may become more robust to unforeseen disruptions (like new urgent jobs or machine failures) later in the planning horizon.

The `FUTURE_WORK_PENALTY_RATE_BASE` is set to `0.1` as a starting point. This value provides a gentle push towards completing longer jobs without drastically altering the primary compute cost and delay penalty considerations.

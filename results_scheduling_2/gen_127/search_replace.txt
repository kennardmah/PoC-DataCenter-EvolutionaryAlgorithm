<DESCRIPTION>
This edit refines the scheduling heuristic by introducing a dynamic search window for local machines based on job priority and re-tuning the impact of job priorities on delay penalties.

1.  **Priority-Based Dynamic Local Search Window**: The maximum time horizon (`MAX_LOCAL_SEARCH_WINDOW`) that the scheduler looks ahead for available local machine slots is now dynamically adjusted based on the job's priority.
    *   'emergency' jobs receive a doubled search window (e.g., `base_window * 2`).
    *   'high' priority jobs receive an extended window (`base_window + inst.time_horizon`).
    *   Other jobs use the base search window.
    This change allows higher-priority jobs to explore a wider range of future local slots to find a better, less costly (or less delayed) placement, increasing their chances of being scheduled on cheaper local resources rather than immediately resorting to cloud, especially during congestion or failures. This balances the need for prompt scheduling with a deeper search for critical tasks.

2.  **Re-tuned Priority Multipliers for Delay Penalties**: The `PRIORITY_MULTIPLIERS` applied to delay penalties have been recalibrated to make 'emergency' and 'high' priority jobs even more sensitive to delays.
    *   'emergency' multiplier increased from `8.0` to `12.0`.
    *   'high' multiplier increased from `3.0` to `5.0`.
    This emphasizes the cost associated with delaying critical jobs, pushing the scheduler to find earlier slots for them, potentially even on cloud resources with their associated (discounted) deterrence costs, if local options are too far in the future. This aligns with prior successful patterns that showed increased sensitivity for high-priority jobs improved overall scores.

        Calculates a weighted score for a proposed operation placement,
        considering compute cost, the impact on the job's overall delay penalty,

        # Calculate the delay penalty for THE ENTIRE JOB.
        delay = max(0, projected_job_completion - _job.deadline)

        # Apply priority multiplier to the delay penalty to reflect job urgency.
        priority_factor = _priority_multipliers.get(_job.priority, 1.0)
        delay_penalty = delay * _job.delay_weight * priority_factor * _inst.weights['delay_penalty']

        # Total score for this specific operation placement.
        total_score = (
            op_compute_cost * _inst.weights['compute_cost']
            + delay_penalty
        )

        # A small positive coefficient for proposed_start ensures that given two options

        total_score += _proposed_start * 0.0001
